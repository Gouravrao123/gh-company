How to Compare Social Media Marketing Agencies, Packages and Paid Social Services

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 6 November 2026

Choosing a social media marketing agency is not just about finding the lowest package price. You need to understand what the agency will actually deliver, how it will manage your accounts, how results will be measured and what happens if you decide to leave. This guide explains how to compare agencies and choose a service that fits your business.

How to compare social media marketing agencies

Two agencies may use similar words in their proposals but offer very different services. One package may include content planning, design, captions and scheduling. Another may include only a limited number of graphics. A third may focus mainly on advertising.

Before comparing prices, ask each agency to explain its process and list the actual work included. A useful comparison should cover these areas:

  1. Scope of work. Check the number and type of posts, reels, stories, platforms and reporting deliverables.
  2. Strategy. Find out whether the agency researches your audience, competitors, offers and business goals before creating content.
  3. Creative quality. Review whether its designs, videos and captions are relevant to your industry and target customers.
  4. Communication. Confirm who manages your account, how approvals work and how quickly questions are answered.
  5. Reporting. Check whether reports explain reach, engagement, profile visits, enquiries and other relevant outcomes.
  6. Ownership. Your business should retain control of its social media pages and advertising accounts.
  7. Contract terms. Review the notice period, payment schedule, renewal terms and exit process.

What are social media management packages?

Social media management packages are service plans that define what an agency will handle for your business over an agreed period, commonly on a monthly basis. They can include content planning, graphic design, video editing, captions, publishing and performance reporting.

However, the phrase "social media management" does not automatically mean every activity is included. Some agencies handle publishing but not customer replies. Others create graphics but require the client to supply all photographs and videos.

What should a social media package include?

A clear proposal should explain the following items before work begins:

  • Platforms: Instagram, Facebook or other agreed channels.
  • Content quantity: The number of static posts, carousels, reels and stories.
  • Content planning: Monthly calendar, themes and campaign ideas.
  • Creative work: Graphic design, video editing, captions and formatting.
  • Publishing: Scheduling, posting and platform-specific formatting.
  • Approvals: How drafts are shared and how many revision rounds are included.
  • Community management: Whether comments and direct messages are monitored or answered.
  • Reporting: The metrics, reporting frequency and review process.
  • Client responsibilities: Photos, videos, product information, approvals and access.
  • Exclusions: Paid ad spend, professional shoots, influencer fees and other additional costs.

What are paid social services?

Paid social services involve using advertising platforms such as Meta Ads to reach selected audiences through paid campaigns. For many local businesses, this can support offers, enquiries, event promotion, admissions or product discovery.

Paid social management may include:

  • Understanding the business goal and campaign objective.
  • Planning the audience and geographic targeting.
  • Preparing ad creatives, copy and calls to action.
  • Setting up campaigns and budgets.
  • Monitoring performance and making appropriate adjustments.
  • Checking available tracking for forms, calls, website actions or messages.
  • Reporting campaign performance and next steps.

A proposal should clearly separate the agency's management fee from the advertising budget. The ad spend is paid to the platform and is not automatically included in the agency's service fee.

Organic social media vs paid social advertising

Organic content and paid advertising serve related but different purposes. Organic content helps your business communicate regularly, demonstrate expertise and build familiarity. Paid advertising can help distribute specific offers or campaigns to selected audiences.

Area Organic social media Paid social advertising
Distribution Content reaches audiences through platform distribution and existing followers Campaigns use an advertising budget to reach selected audiences
Main activities Posts, reels, stories, community interaction and content planning Campaign setup, audience selection, ad creative and optimisation
Costs Content creation and management costs may apply Management fees plus separate advertising spend
Useful for Consistency, brand familiarity, education and trust Promoting offers, reaching selected audiences and testing campaigns
Measurement Reach, engagement, profile visits, messages and enquiries Spend, impressions, clicks, leads and tracked conversions

A business does not always need both services immediately. Start with the activity that best supports your current goal, budget and ability to follow up with potential customers.

Questions to ask before hiring a social media agency

A proposal meeting should help you understand how the agency thinks, not just what it sells. Ask practical questions and request clear answers.

  1. What would your first month look like for my business? Look for a plan based on your goals, customers and existing online presence.
  2. What exactly will you deliver each month? Ask for quantities, formats, platforms and deadlines.
  3. Who will work on my account? Understand who handles strategy, design, editing, publishing and communication.
  4. How will content approvals work? Confirm how drafts are shared and how revisions are handled.
  5. How will you measure performance? Ask which metrics matter for your business and how enquiries will be tracked.
  6. Is advertising included? Clarify whether paid campaign management and platform ad spend are separate.
  7. Who owns the accounts and creative files? Confirm access, ownership and handover terms in writing.
  8. What are the payment and cancellation terms? Check the billing schedule, notice period and any minimum commitment.

Warning signs to watch for

Be careful if an agency avoids explaining its process or gives you a proposal that is difficult to evaluate. These are reasons to ask more questions before committing:

  • Promises of guaranteed followers, viral posts, leads or sales.
  • Unclear monthly content quantities or vague deliverables.
  • No written agreement about fees, revisions or additional work.
  • Reports focused only on likes without connecting activity to business goals.
  • Refusal to explain who owns the business accounts.
  • Pressure to pay before the agency understands your requirements.
  • Unclear advertising budgets or hidden third-party charges.
  • No practical explanation of how the team will communicate with you.

These signs do not automatically prove that an agency is unsuitable, but they deserve clarification. You can also read our guide on what to check in agency contracts before signing.

How GH Company approaches social media management

At GH Company, the starting point is understanding what the business wants to achieve. A school may want more relevant admission enquiries, while a local retailer may want more product discovery and store visits. Their content plans should not be identical.

A practical workflow should include:

  1. Understand the business. Review its services, audience, location, goals and current online presence.

Frequently asked questions

What do social media management packages include?

Packages may include content planning, a defined number of posts or reels, graphic design, captions, scheduling and reporting. Confirm the exact monthly quantities, revision limits and exclusions in writing.

Is ad spend included in social media agency fees?

Usually not. Ad spend is paid to the advertising platform, while the agency fee covers agreed campaign planning, setup, management and reporting. Always ask for a separate breakdown.

How do I compare social media marketing agencies?

Compare deliverables, strategy, communication, reporting, account ownership, contract terms and total costs. Ask each agency to provide a written scope so you can compare like for like.

Should I hire an agency for organic social media or paid ads?

It depends on your goal. Organic social media supports content, visibility and audience relationships, while paid ads use a budget to reach selected audiences. Some businesses need one service first and others benefit from both.

Who should own my social media and advertising accounts?

Your business should normally retain ownership of its social media pages, business portfolio and advertising accounts. The agency can receive the access needed to perform its work. Confirm ownership and handover arrangements in writing.
