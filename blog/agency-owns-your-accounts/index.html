Who Owns Your Business Accounts? Protect Your Domain, Ads and Digital Assets

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 4 November 2026 · Updated 14 November 2026

Your website, Google Business Profile, social media pages and advertising accounts are valuable business assets. But what happens if the agency managing them controls the ownership? Learn how to keep your business in control while still giving your agency the access it needs.

Key takeaways

  • Your business should retain control of its important digital assets.
  • An agency usually needs the right permissions to work, not unrestricted ownership.
  • Keep domain registration, recovery email addresses and renewal details under business control.
  • Use platform roles and invitations instead of sharing your main passwords wherever possible.
  • Document ownership, access, deliverables and handover responsibilities in writing.
  • Review account access regularly, including when an employee or agency relationship ends.

What does an agency controlling your accounts look like?

Imagine paying an agency to build your website, manage your social media and run advertising campaigns. The work is going well, but you have no direct access to the accounts behind it.

Common warning signs include:

  • Your domain registrar account is registered using an agency employee's email address.
  • You cannot access your website hosting dashboard or request a backup independently.
  • Your Google Business Profile has no business-controlled primary owner.
  • Your Meta Business Portfolio, ad account or other advertising assets are controlled by someone outside your business.
  • Your social media pages are connected to a personal account you cannot access.
  • Website files, designs, videos or other paid deliverables are stored only in an agency-controlled workspace.
  • You cannot see campaign history, invoices, reports or account permissions without asking the agency.

One warning sign does not automatically mean an agency is acting dishonestly. However, unclear ownership and restricted access should be resolved before they become a serious problem.

Ownership and access are not the same thing

This distinction is important. A person can have permission to manage an account without being its owner. Similarly, an agency may need administrator-level permissions for a particular task, but that does not automatically mean the agency should control the underlying business asset.

For example, a business can retain ownership or primary control of a Google Business Profile and invite an agency to manage it. A website developer can work on a website using delegated hosting or CMS access without taking control of the domain registration.

Exact roles and transfer options vary by platform. Check the official platform settings and transfer procedures instead of assuming that every account works in the same way.

Why does this happen?

There are several reasons an agency might create or manage accounts under its own login:

  • Convenience: Creating accounts through an existing agency workspace can seem faster.
  • Lack of process: Some agencies do not have a clear onboarding and account ownership checklist.
  • Client inexperience: Business owners may not know which accounts should be registered in their own name.
  • Staff turnover: Accounts created using an employee's personal email can become difficult to manage when that employee leaves.
  • Unclear agreements: Contracts may not explain who controls accounts, files, licences or access after the project ends.

The solution is not to block agencies from doing their work. It is to establish clear ownership and provide the appropriate permissions from the beginning.

What can your business lose?

If important accounts are controlled by someone else, changing agencies or bringing marketing in-house can become unnecessarily complicated.

  • Website disruption: You may struggle to renew your domain, change hosting or access website files.
  • Advertising interruption: You may lose visibility into campaign settings, billing or historical performance.
  • Local search disruption: Difficulty accessing your Google Business Profile can affect your ability to manage business information and respond to reviews.
  • Social media problems: You may be unable to manage page permissions, connected accounts or published content.
  • Extra expenses: Rebuilding assets or recovering access can require additional time and money.
  • Business dependency: Routine changes may require the agency's involvement even after the relationship has ended.

Not every platform allows every asset to be transferred freely. Some account histories, permissions, licences or platform-specific features may have restrictions. That is why ownership and handover terms should be discussed before work begins.

Which digital assets should your business control?

Use this checklist when reviewing your digital marketing setup. The exact ownership structure may differ depending on the platform, business type and agreement, but your business should have clear control and documented access to the assets it has paid for.

Digital asset What your business should check
Domain name Business-controlled registrar account, registrant details, renewal date and recovery email.
Website hosting Access to the hosting account, billing details, backups and migration information.
Website and CMS Administrator access, website files, database backups and agreed rights to the delivered work.
Google Business Profile Business-controlled primary ownership and appropriate agency manager access.
Meta Business Portfolio Business control of relevant assets, people, partner permissions and security settings.
Advertising accounts Access to the relevant ad account, billing arrangements, campaign data and reporting.
Social media pages Business-controlled access, recovery options and authorised administrators.
Analytics and tracking Access to analytics properties, tracking configurations and available historical reports.
Creative files Delivery of agreed final designs, videos, source files where included and usage rights.

How to protect your business accounts

  1. Create an asset inventory. List every domain, hosting account, website, social media page, Google profile, advertising account, analytics property and creative workspace used by your business.
  2. Keep ownership details under business control. Use a business-controlled email address and reliable recovery details for important accounts. Make sure renewal notices and billing information reach the appropriate person.
  3. Give the agency appropriate access. Use manager, employee, partner or other platform-supported permissions where available. Give only the access needed to complete the agreed work.
  4. Secure your accounts. Enable two-factor authentication, use strong unique passwords and keep recovery codes in a secure place. Avoid sharing the main account password when user invitations are available.
  5. Document deliverables and rights. Your agreement should explain what files will be delivered, who owns or may use the work, what licences apply and whether editable or source files are included.
  6. Keep backups and records. Maintain website backups, important creative files, invoices, campaign reports and relevant account information in a business-controlled location.
  7. Agree on an exit process. Document how access will be reviewed, how deliverables will be handed over and how agency permissions will be removed when the relationship ends.
  8. Review access periodically. Check active users, connected partners, recovery information and account ownership at regular intervals.

Questions to ask before hiring a digital marketing agency

You do not need to be technically advanced to ask these questions. A professional agency should be able to explain its account access and handover process clearly.

  • Who will register and control my domain name?
  • Will my business retain access to website hosting and the CMS?
  • Will I remain the primary owner of my Google Business Profile?
  • Who controls the Meta Business Portfolio and advertising assets?
  • Can I see campaign performance and billing information directly?
  • Will I receive copies of final designs, videos and agreed website files?
  • What happens to my accounts and permissions if I stop working with you?
  • How long will the handover take, and what will be included?
  • Are there any third-party subscriptions, licences or renewal costs I should know about?

A simple example: a salon's Instagram account

Imagine a salon hires an agency to manage its Instagram presence. The page is created using an employee's personal login, and the salon owner is never given appropriate administrative access.

Later, the employee leaves the agency. The salon owner now has difficulty managing the page, changing permissions or recovering access.

A better arrangement would have established business-controlled access from the beginning, with the agency added through the platform's available permission system.

Common account ownership mistakes

  • Sharing passwords casually: Use individual user access and permissions wherever supported.
  • Ignoring domain renewals: A domain can expire if renewal notices go to an email nobody checks.
  • Not checking primary ownership: Having the ability to edit a profile does not necessarily mean you control its ownership.
  • Confusing an ad account with a Business Portfolio: Review the ownership and permissions of each relevant asset separately.
  • Forgetting website backups: A live website alone is not a substitute for a recoverable backup.
  • Leaving everything in one employee's account: Use business-controlled recovery details and review staff access when roles change.
  • Skipping the contract: Agree on asset ownership, usage rights, handover and outstanding costs in writing.

Digital asset ownership: myths vs reality

Myth Reality
The agency must own my account to manage it. Many platforms support delegated roles and permissions.
If I can see the account, I own it. Viewing or editing access does not always mean ownership or primary control.
Only the website domain matters. Advertising, social, analytics, hosting and creative assets also need clear access arrangements.
The agency will handle everything if I leave. A written handover process makes responsibilities and expectations clearer.
Account security is only a technical issue. Ownership, permissions and recovery details are also business management responsibilities.

A practical 30-day account ownership action plan

  1. Week 1 — Audit: List all important digital assets, current owners, users, recovery emails and renewal dates.
  2. Week 2 — Resolve: Identify missing business access and discuss the appropriate ownership or transfer process with the current agency.
  3. Week 3 — Secure: Review permissions, enable two-factor authentication and organise backups and important records.
  4. Week 4 — Document: Record the final ownership and access arrangements, agree on handover responsibilities and schedule a quarterly review.

Do not remove an existing administrator or change account ownership without checking the platform's process and ensuring that your business has secure access. A rushed change can interrupt services or create a recovery problem.

How do you know your business is protected?

Your account ownership process is in a healthier position when:

  • You know who owns or controls every important digital asset.
  • An authorised business representative can access the required owner or administrator settings.
  • Recovery email addresses and security methods are current.
  • Your agency has only the permissions needed for its work.
  • You can access agreed reports, backups and final creative deliverables.
  • There is a documented process for ending the agency relationship.

The goal is simple: your business should not become dependent on one agency or employee just to access the digital assets it relies on.

Frequently asked questions

Who should own the business domain?

The business or its authorised owner should control the domain registration, registrar account and renewal details. An agency can receive delegated access to manage the website.

Can an agency manage my Google Business Profile without owning it?

Yes. The business can retain primary ownership and add the agency as a manager or another appropriate user through Google's access settings.

Should I share my passwords with a digital marketing agency?

Where a platform supports roles or user invitations, use those instead of sharing the main password. Enable two-factor authentication and keep recovery details under business control.

What should I do if my agency already controls my accounts?

Make an asset inventory, review the platform's ownership and access settings, and request an agreed transfer or appropriate owner access in writing. Follow each platform's official transfer process.
