Agency Slow Replies and a Disappearing Account Manager: What to Do

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 4 November 2026

The sales team was quick and friendly. After payment, messages take days. Here is how to spot and prevent this before you commit.

Key takeaways

  • Ask who your contact will be
  • Agree a response time
  • Choose one channel
  • Test responsiveness during discovery

What this frustration looks like

  • Messages stay unanswered for a long time.
  • You are passed between people who do not know your project.
  • Meetings are postponed.
  • Problems are solved only after you chase repeatedly.

Why it happens

Agencies grow faster than their processes. Account managers handle too many clients, and nobody owns the relationship. Sometimes the person who sold the service is not the person who delivers it.

What it costs you

Slow communication delays approvals, campaigns and fixes. It also signals how the agency may behave when something important goes wrong.

How to avoid it

  1. Ask who your contact will be and whether you can meet them before signing.
  2. Agree a response time, such as one working day for messages.
  3. Choose one channel for important decisions, such as WhatsApp or email.
  4. Test responsiveness during discovery. It rarely improves after payment.
  5. Ask about escalation: who to contact if your account manager is unavailable.
  6. Schedule fixed review calls so communication is routine.

Questions to ask any agency

  • Who is my main contact and who is the backup?
  • How quickly do you usually reply?
  • How will urgent issues be handled?
  • How often will we meet or speak?

A concept example

A clinic waits four days for a simple ad approval. A fixed weekly check-in and a named backup contact would have kept the campaign running. This is an illustration, not a client result.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Judging only by the sales conversation.
  • Not noting response times from the start.
  • Accepting delays repeatedly without raising them.
  • Having no written communication plan.

Myths vs reality

MythReality
Busy agencies are good agenciesBusy agencies still need processes for clients
Slow replies mean quality workThey usually mean overload
Complaining will harm the relationshipClear, calm feedback helps both sides

A 30-day action plan

  1. Week 1: Note the reply time of every agency you speak with.
  2. Week 2: Ask for a named contact and backup.
  3. Week 3: Set response expectations in writing.
  4. Week 4: Review communication after the first month.

How to measure improvement

Track time to first reply and the number of follow-ups you need. If most messages are answered within the agreed time, communication is healthy.

Frequently asked questions

What is a reasonable reply time?

Within one working day for normal messages is common. Urgent issues should have a faster route.

Should I meet my account manager before signing?

If possible, yes. A short call shows how they communicate.

What if my account manager leaves?

Ask in advance how handovers work so nothing is lost.
