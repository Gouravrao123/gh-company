2. How long before I see results?

Paid ads can bring visits within days of launching, but quality improves over several weeks as the campaign is tested and refined. Social media usually needs a few months of consistent posting before it builds familiarity. SEO is the slowest, often taking several months before competitive pages move. A fair agency will tell you which parts of your plan are quick and which are slow.

A quick PPC advertising guide

PPC means pay-per-click advertising: you create ads and pay the platform, depending on the platform and settings, when people see or click them. Common places are Google Search, YouTube, Facebook and Instagram. A sensible first campaign follows these steps:

  1. Pick one goal, such as calls, WhatsApp chats or form leads.
  2. Choose a small local audience or a handful of focused keywords.
  3. Write one clear offer and prepare two or three creatives or ad texts.
  4. Send people to a page or WhatsApp number that is ready for them.
  5. Set up tracking before the ad goes live.
  6. Review weekly, pause what does not work and scale slowly.

Usually yes. Social platforms change rules and reach without notice, while a website is yours. It also appears in Google searches, carries your detailed service information and gives customers one reliable place to contact you. A simple, fast, mobile-friendly site is enough to begin. 5. How much should I spend on ads?

Start with an amount you can afford to lose while you learn, and decide in advance what result would justify spending more. Divide your test budget across a few weeks rather than a few days. Treat ad budget and agency fee as separate lines.

9. How will I know it is working?

Look at enquiries first: calls, WhatsApp chats, forms and visits. Then check supporting numbers such as Google Business Profile actions, Search Console impressions and clicks, and website visits. Ask for a simple monthly summary that shows what was done and what happens next.

10. Do I need to post every day?

No. Consistency and usefulness matter more than daily posting. Choose a rhythm you can keep for at least 90 days, and make each post answer a customer question, show real work or make a clear offer. Frequently asked questions

What is the first thing a small business should do for digital marketing?

Complete your Google Business Profile, make sure your website works well on mobile, and write one clear sentence about who you help and how. Then choose one or two channels to focus on.

Can I do digital marketing myself?

Yes, many owners start themselves, especially with Google Business Profile and WhatsApp. When time or skills run short, a partner can take over planning, creative work and reporting.
