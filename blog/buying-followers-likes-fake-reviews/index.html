Mistake: Buying Followers, Likes or Fake Reviews

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 4 November 2026

Fake numbers feel like a shortcut to credibility. In reality they damage trust, mislead you and can break platform rules.

Key takeaways

  • Focus on real local people
  • Ask satisfied customers for honest reviews
  • Reply to every review
  • Share real stories

What this mistake looks like

  • Followers jump but engagement stays flat.
  • Reviews sound alike or come from unknown profiles.
  • Comments are generic and from unrelated accounts.
  • Real customers cannot find genuine proof among the noise.

Why businesses make it

Social proof matters, and building it honestly takes time. Competitors seem to have more followers and reviews, which creates pressure to catch up quickly.

What it costs you

Fake followers do not buy, share or reply. Fake reviews can be detected, can break platform policies and, if discovered, damage your reputation far more than having few reviews. They also make your real performance impossible to read.

What to do instead

  1. Focus on real local people, even if the number is smaller.
  2. Ask satisfied customers for honest reviews using a simple link.
  3. Reply to every review politely.
  4. Share real stories and work with permission.
  5. Measure enquiries, not follower counts.
  6. Remove or report suspicious activity.

A quick self-check

  • Are my followers from my area and audience?
  • Are reviews specific and genuine?
  • Do I know how each review was obtained?
  • Are my numbers helping me decide?

A concept example

A salon stops chasing followers and asks every happy customer for a review after the service. Its numbers grow slowly but are real and bring bookings. This is an illustration, not a client result.

Related mistakes

  • Offering rewards for positive reviews.
  • Asking friends and family to post fake feedback.
  • Using engagement pods.
  • Hiding negative reviews.

What to do if you already bought followers

Stop buying, do not add more, and focus on engagement from real people. You may remove suspicious followers where the platform allows. Review your numbers with a realistic baseline, so you can judge new content fairly.

For reviews, never delete honest negatives. Reply calmly, ask genuine customers to share their experience and be patient. Credibility returns gradually when behaviour changes.

Myths vs reality

MythReality
More followers means more trustReal engagement matters
Competitors do it so I mustTheir risk is not your advantage
One fake review will not matterIt undermines everything

A 30-day action plan

  1. Week 1: Audit your followers and reviews.
  2. Week 2: Stop any paid fake activity.
  3. Week 3: Create a polite review-request message.
  4. Week 4: Share three real customer stories with permission.

How to measure improvement

Watch genuine reviews, saves, messages and enquiries rather than total followers.

Frequently asked questions

Is it wrong to ask for reviews?

No, asking politely is fine. Do not offer rewards for positive wording.

What if a competitor has fake reviews?

Report them through the platform and focus on your own honesty.

How do I handle negative reviews?

Reply calmly, apologise where appropriate and offer to help.
