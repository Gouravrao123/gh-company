Not Knowing What Your Agency Is Doing: How to Get Clear, Useful Reporting

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 4 November 2026

A monthly report should make you feel informed, not confused. If it does not, here is what to ask for.

What it looks like

  • The report is full of reach, impressions and charts but no enquiries.
  • You do not know which tasks were done.
  • Questions get vague answers.
  • There is no clear plan for next month.

Why it happens

Reporting is often built around what is easy to export from a platform, not what the business needs to know. Sometimes scope was never written clearly, so there is nothing to report against.

What a useful report contains

SectionWhat it answers
SummaryWhat happened this month in plain language?
Work doneWhich tasks and deliverables were completed?
ResultsHow many calls, messages, forms or visits came in, and from where?
SpendIf ads are involved, what was spent and what did it deliver?
LearningsWhat worked, what did not, and why?
Next monthWhat will change and what do you need to decide?

How to fix it

  1. Write the scope down. Deliverables, quantities, timelines and reporting dates.
  2. Agree the metrics first. Choose three to five numbers that matter to your business.
  3. Ask for a short call after each report to explain it.
  4. Ask to see the work itself. Posts, pages, campaigns and access to accounts you own.
  5. Compare month to month, not only against a target.
  6. Raise concerns early. A good partner welcomes questions.

Warning signs

  • Reports sent late, or not at all.
  • Only vanity metrics are shown.
  • You cannot access your own accounts.
  • Promises of guaranteed results.

Why clear reporting protects both sides

Good reporting is not only for the client. It helps the agency focus on the right work and helps you decide with confidence. When both sides look at the same numbers and agree what they mean, disagreements fall and trust grows.

Questions to ask in every review call

  • What did you do this month, and why?
  • What results came from that work?
  • What did not work, and what did you learn?
  • What will you change next month?
  • What do you need from me?

Three scenarios (concepts)

  • A report full of reach. Asking for enquiries by source reveals what the numbers really mean.
  • No report at all. Setting a fixed reporting date turns a vague service into an accountable one.
  • Unclear tasks. A shared task list shows what has been done and what is pending.

Myths about reports

  • "A longer report means more work." Short, clear reports are often better.
  • "Numbers are enough." Numbers need explanation and decisions.
  • "Only the agency understands them." A good agency explains in plain language.

A 30-day action plan

  1. Week 1: write down your goals and the three to five numbers that matter.
  2. Week 2: request a scope document and a sample report format.
  3. Week 3: hold your first review call using the questions above.
  4. Week 4: agree on changes and set the next reporting date.

How to measure improvement

You will know reporting is working when you can explain, in two minutes, what was done last month, what it brought and what comes next.

Frequently asked questions

What numbers should an agency report?

Choose a few that match your goals, such as enquiries, calls, messages, direction requests, website visits and, for ads, cost per result.

How often should I get a report?

Monthly is common, with quick updates in between for active campaigns.

Should I have access to my own accounts?

Yes. Your business should own its accounts, with the agency added as a manager or user.
