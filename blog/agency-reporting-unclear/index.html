Digital Marketing Agency Reports: What to Check and What to Ask

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 4 November 2026

You are paying for digital marketing, but can you explain what your agency actually did last month? A useful monthly report should show completed work, meaningful results, what those results mean for your business and what happens next.

Signs your digital marketing agency reporting is unclear

A report can look professional and still fail to answer the most important question: what value did the marketing work create for the business?

Look out for these common problems:

  • The report is full of reach, impressions and charts but does not explain enquiries or business outcomes.
  • You cannot identify which posts, campaigns, pages or other deliverables were completed.
  • You receive screenshots without context or an explanation of what changed.
  • You do not know whether reported enquiries came from organic activity, paid advertising, referrals or another source.
  • Your questions receive vague answers instead of clear explanations.
  • There is no action plan or measurable priority for the next month.

One weak month does not automatically mean an agency is doing poor work. However, repeated reporting gaps make it difficult to judge performance, understand decisions and plan your marketing budget.

Why digital marketing agency reports become unclear

Reporting is often built around what is easy to export from a platform instead of what a business needs to understand. Social media platforms, advertising dashboards and website analytics tools all measure different activities.

Common reasons include:

  • No agreed scope: Deliverables, timelines and responsibilities were never clearly documented.
  • Wrong reporting priorities: The agency focuses on available platform statistics instead of business objectives.
  • Missing tracking: Calls, forms, WhatsApp messages or sales are not consistently attributed to marketing sources.
  • No baseline: There is no earlier period or starting point for comparison.
  • Weak communication: The report is sent without a meeting or plain-language explanation.

The solution is not necessarily a longer report. It is a report that connects agreed work to relevant performance indicators and explains what can reasonably be concluded from the available data.

What a useful monthly agency report should contain

A good report should help you understand six things: what was planned, what was completed, what happened, how much was spent, what was learned and what will happen next.

Report section What it should answer
Executive summary What happened this month, in simple language?
Work completed Which agreed tasks and deliverables were completed?
Performance results What happened to relevant traffic, engagement, enquiries or conversions?
Lead sources Where did tracked calls, forms, messages or other enquiries originate?
Advertising spend How much was spent, what results were tracked and what was the cost per result?
Learnings What worked, what did not and what could explain the outcome?
Next month's plan What will change, what will continue and what decisions are needed?

The report should also identify the reporting period, data sources and any limitations. For example, if WhatsApp enquiries are not tracked through a configured system, the agency should not present an estimated number as a verified conversion count.

Which digital marketing metrics actually matter?

Not every business needs the same dashboard. A coaching institute, a local retailer and an e-commerce business may have completely different marketing objectives.

Start with a small set of metrics that match your actual goal.

Business goal Useful metrics to review
Generate enquiries Qualified leads, calls, forms, WhatsApp enquiries and enquiry sources
Improve local visibility Relevant local search visibility, website visits from organic search and available business profile interactions
Grow social media presence Reach, relevant engagement, profile visits, audience growth and content performance
Run paid advertising Advertising spend, impressions, clicks, tracked conversions and cost per result
Improve website performance Relevant visits, landing-page engagement, tracked form submissions and conversion rate

Understand the difference between activity and outcomes

  • Reach: The number of unique accounts that saw content, as defined by the platform.
  • Impressions: The number of times content was displayed.
  • Clicks: The number of times users clicked a link or advertisement.
  • Enquiries: People who contacted your business through a measurable channel.
  • Conversions: Actions that you have defined and configured for tracking, such as a completed form or purchase.
  • Cost per lead: Advertising spend divided by the number of leads counted under the agreed definition.

High reach does not automatically mean high sales. Similarly, low website traffic does not tell the full story if a campaign is generating relevant enquiries through another channel. Always connect the metric to the business objective.

How to fix unclear agency reporting

  1. Write the scope down. List deliverables, quantities, timelines, responsibilities and reporting dates.
  2. Agree on metrics first. Choose three to five key numbers that reflect your business goals.
  3. Define how leads are counted. Agree on what qualifies as an enquiry, duplicate lead or conversion.
  4. Ask for a short review call. Discuss the report instead of relying only on a PDF or screenshot.
  5. Ask to see the actual work. Review posts, pages, campaigns and completed deliverables.
  6. Compare performance over time. Review trends and context instead of judging one isolated number.
  7. Agree on the next action plan. Every report should lead to practical decisions and clear priorities.
  8. Raise concerns early. A professional working relationship should allow reasonable questions and honest discussions.

Warning signs in an agency's reporting process

  • Reports are repeatedly late or are not shared at all.
  • Only vanity metrics are presented without explaining their relevance.
  • The agency cannot identify which agreed deliverables were completed.
  • Advertising spend and agency fees are mixed together without a clear breakdown.
  • There is no explanation of how enquiries or conversions are counted.
  • You cannot access your own business accounts or see relevant campaign data.
  • Questions about performance are avoided or answered with unsupported promises.
  • The agency guarantees rankings, leads or sales without explaining the limitations and conditions.

A single missed report or disappointing result is not enough to judge an entire agency relationship. Look for repeated patterns, ask for clarification and compare the service against the written agreement.

Why clear reporting protects both the client and agency

Good reporting is not only for the client. It helps the agency focus on the right work, identify problems early and make better decisions.

When both sides agree on the scope, metrics and reporting process:

  • Expectations become easier to manage.
  • Completed and pending work can be identified.
  • Performance discussions become more objective.
  • Budget decisions can be based on clearer information.
  • Both sides can identify what needs to improve.
  • There is less confusion about responsibilities and deliverables.

A transparent report does not guarantee success. It gives both parties a better basis for evaluating progress and deciding what to do next.

Questions to ask in every monthly review call

Keep these questions handy when discussing your digital marketing performance:

  • What did you complete this month, and where can I see the work?
  • Which activities contributed to our main business objective?
  • How many enquiries were recorded, and how were they counted?
  • Which channels generated those enquiries?
  • What was the advertising spend, separate from your management fee?
  • What did not work as expected, and what did you learn?
  • What will you change next month, and why?
  • What do you need from me to move forward?
  • Are there any tracking gaps or data limitations I should know about?

Three practical scenarios: how to handle unclear reports

1. A report full of reach and impressions

Your agency shares a social media report showing a large increase in reach. However, you cannot tell whether anyone visited your profile, contacted your business or made an enquiry.

What to ask: Can you show profile visits, relevant engagement and tracked enquiries alongside reach? If enquiries are not tracked, what setup would help measure them?

2. No report is being shared

You receive occasional updates on WhatsApp but no structured monthly summary. You have no clear record of completed work or upcoming tasks.

What to ask: Can we agree on a fixed monthly reporting date and a simple format covering completed work, results, learnings and next steps?

3. You cannot identify completed tasks

Your monthly invoice arrives, but you cannot tell which posts, campaigns, website updates or other deliverables were completed.

What to ask: Can we maintain a shared task tracker with planned, completed and pending work, including links to the actual deliverables?

These are illustrative situations, not actual client case studies or guaranteed outcomes.

Common myths about digital marketing reports

  • "A longer report means more work was done." Not necessarily. A short, clear report can communicate performance more effectively than dozens of unexplained charts.
  • "More followers automatically mean more customers." Audience growth can be useful, but it does not guarantee enquiries or sales.
  • "Numbers alone explain performance." Numbers need context, comparison and an explanation of what should happen next.
  • "Only the agency can understand the data." A good agency should explain relevant metrics in straightforward language.
  • "A good report guarantees good results." Reporting improves visibility and decision-making; it does not guarantee business outcomes.

A 30-day action plan for better agency accountability

  1. Week 1 — Define expectations: Write down your business goals, agreed services and three to five important metrics.
  2. Week 2 — Review reporting: Request a sample report format, completed deliverables and details of data sources.
  3. Week 3 — Hold a review call: Discuss results, tracking limitations, missed tasks and the questions in this guide.
  4. Week 4 — Agree on improvements: Set priorities, assign responsibilities and confirm the next reporting date.

Keep a copy of the agreed scope and monthly reports. This makes it easier to compare performance and understand whether the service is meeting expectations.

How to measure whether reporting is improving

Your reporting process is becoming more useful when you can answer these questions without guessing:

  • What work was completed during the reporting period?
  • Which business goals were being targeted?
  • What results were recorded, and where did the data come from?
  • What could not be measured reliably?
  • What did the agency learn from the results?
  • What will happen next month?

A simple test: you should be able to explain, in two minutes, what was done last month, what the available data shows and what the next priorities are.

Frequently asked questions

What should a digital marketing agency include in its monthly report?

A useful report should include a plain-language summary, completed work, relevant performance metrics, enquiries or conversions where tracking is available, advertising spend if applicable, key learnings and the next month's action plan.

Which marketing metrics should a small business track?

Choose metrics based on your business goals. Common examples include qualified enquiries, calls, WhatsApp messages, website visits, conversion rate, cost per lead for paid campaigns and relevant local actions.

How often should a marketing agency send reports?

Monthly reporting is common for ongoing marketing services. Active advertising campaigns may also need shorter performance updates, depending on the agreed scope.

What should I do if my agency report is unclear?

Ask for a plain-language explanation, a record of completed deliverables, the source of reported results and a clear action plan. Agree on reporting metrics and dates in writing.

Should my business have access to its marketing accounts?

Your business should generally retain ownership and appropriate administrative access to its digital assets. An agency can be granted the permissions it needs to perform its work.
