Agency Using the Same Template for Every Client: How to Tell and What to Do

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 4 November 2026

A gym, a school and a hardware shop receive the same posts with a new logo. Here is how to spot template marketing and ask for a real plan.

Key takeaways

  • Ask how your plan differs
  • Share your customers, offers and competitors
  • Request a content plan
  • Review samples

What this frustration looks like

  • Your posts look like they could belong to any business.
  • The same festival creatives are used for every client.
  • The plan does not mention your customers or location.
  • Keywords and captions are generic.

Why it happens

Templates are cheap and fast. When agencies take on too many clients, standard packages replace strategy.

What it costs you

Generic content does not stand out. It rarely speaks to your customers, so it earns few saves, messages or enquiries.

How to avoid it

  1. Ask how your plan differs from the plan for another industry.
  2. Share your customers, offers and competitors in a short brief.
  3. Request a content plan that mentions your location and services.
  4. Review samples for specific details, not generic quotes.
  5. Keep some templates, such as brand layouts, but customise messages.
  6. Give feedback early on what feels generic.

Questions to ask any agency

  • How will my plan be different from your other clients?
  • What research will you do on my customers?
  • Can I see a sample plan?
  • How do you adapt content for my city?

A concept example

A coaching centre receives “Happy Diwali” posters used by every client. A tailored plan would explain batches, results process and parent questions instead. This is an illustration, not a client result.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Accepting packages without a brief.
  • Never giving feedback.
  • Confusing brand templates with generic content.
  • Ignoring local context.

Myths vs reality

MythReality
Templates guarantee consistencyBrand templates help, but messages must be tailored
Custom work is always expensiveA short brief often makes content more relevant at little cost
My industry is the same as othersLocal customers and offers differ

A 30-day action plan

  1. Week 1: Write a one-page brief about your customers and offers.
  2. Week 2: Ask for a sample plan based on it.
  3. Week 3: Review for specifics.
  4. Week 4: Adjust and approve.

How to measure improvement

You will know it is working when your posts could only belong to your business, and customers mention them.

Frequently asked questions

Is using templates always bad?

No. Layout templates keep branding consistent. The problem is generic messages.

How do I give a good brief?

Describe customers, offers, competitors, location and goals in a page.

Can a package still be personalised?

Yes, if the agency adapts content to your brief.
