Ad Spend With No Tracking: How to Know What Your Money Is Doing

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 4 November 2026

Boosted posts, ad campaigns and agency fees all need one thing in common: a way to count what came back. Without it, every month is a guess.

What it looks like

  • You boost posts and see likes, but cannot connect them to customers.
  • You cannot say how many calls or messages came from which source.
  • Reports show reach and impressions only.
  • Decisions are made on feelings.

Why it happens

Tracking feels technical, and setting it up is easy to postpone. Also, people often start spending the same day they decide to advertise. By the time results are needed, nobody remembers how to find where each enquiry came from.

What it costs you

Without tracking you cannot stop wasting money on what does not work or put more behind what does. Two campaigns can look the same on the surface while one brings customers and the other brings only clicks.

How to set up simple tracking

  1. Define a result. A call, a WhatsApp message, a form, a visit or an admission. Pick one primary result per campaign.
  2. Ask every new customer one question: "How did you find us?" Record the answers in a simple sheet.
  3. Use separate links or numbers for different campaigns where possible so you can tell them apart.
  4. Check platform reports weekly for cost per result, not only reach.
  5. Connect your website to Google Search Console to see searches that lead to your pages.
  6. Review monthly: what did we spend, what did we get, what will we change?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Judging a campaign after two days.
  • Changing audience, creative and budget at once.
  • Counting likes as results.
  • Forgetting offline sources such as walk-ins who saw the ad.

Why "it feels like it is working" is not enough

Busy weeks and festival seasons bring enquiries regardless of advertising. Without records, it is impossible to tell whether your ad or your season did the work. Tracking separates the two and protects your budget from habits and assumptions.

Numbers worth knowing

MeasureWhat it tells you
Cost per enquiryHow much you spent for each call, message or form
Enquiry to customer rateHow many enquiries became paying customers
Cost per customerTotal spend divided by the customers you gained
Return on spendWhether the revenue from those customers justified the cost

You do not need all of them on day one. Start with cost per enquiry, then add the others as your records improve.

Three scenarios (concepts)

  • A salon boosting posts. It gets likes but few bookings. Switching the goal to WhatsApp messages and counting bookings shows the real picture.
  • A coaching centre running two ads. One gets cheaper clicks, the other more enquiries. Tracking shows which one deserves the budget.
  • A clinic with a new landing page. Counting calls from the page shows whether the redesign helped.

Myths about ad results

  • "More reach means more customers." Reach measures visibility, not intent.
  • "The platform will do it all." Platforms optimise for what you tell them to count.
  • "Tracking is for big companies." A notebook is a tracking system.

A 30-day action plan

  1. Week 1: decide your primary result and create your tracking sheet.
  2. Week 2: launch a small test with a clear goal and record daily or weekly numbers.
  3. Week 3: compare creatives or audiences. Pause the weakest, keep the strongest.
  4. Week 4: calculate cost per enquiry and decide the next month's budget.

How to measure improvement

Your goal is not a perfect number but a clearer picture each month: which campaigns bring enquiries, what they cost and which ones deserve more or less budget.

Frequently asked questions

What should I track first?

Track enquiries: calls, messages and forms. Then connect them to the source, such as ad, search, social post or referral.

Can I track phone calls from ads?

Yes, through call buttons, separate numbers or by asking each caller how they found you. Start with the simplest method you will actually use.

How long should I run an ad before judging it?

Give it enough time to gather data, usually a couple of weeks, unless it is clearly not working. Avoid changing many things at once.
