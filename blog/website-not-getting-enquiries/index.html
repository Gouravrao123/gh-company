A Website That Brings No Enquiries: Why Visitors Leave and What to Fix

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 4 November 2026

You paid for a website, it looks fine, and still the phone stays quiet. The problem is rarely the colours. It is usually clarity, speed and the next step.

What it looks like

  • Visitors arrive but few call, message or fill the form.
  • The phone number is hard to find on mobile.
  • Pages say "we provide quality services" without saying what the service is.
  • The site loads slowly or looks broken on small screens.

Why it happens

Many websites are built as a brochure, not as a tool for getting enquiries. They describe the company but do not answer the visitor's questions: Can you help me? What does it cost? Where are you? How do I contact you?

What it costs you

Every ad, post and search result sends people to the site. If the site loses them, you pay again to bring the next visitor. Fixing the website often improves every other channel at once.

Eight fixes, in order of impact

  1. Say what you do in the first screen. A clear headline, one supporting line and one button.
  2. Show your contact options everywhere. Call and WhatsApp buttons that stay visible on mobile.
  3. Give each service its own page with who it is for, what is included and what to do next.
  4. Add proof you can stand behind. Real photos, genuine reviews and clear process. No invented testimonials.
  5. Answer common questions with a short FAQ, including price guidance where possible.
  6. Make it fast on mobile. Compress images, avoid heavy sliders and test on an ordinary connection.
  7. Make the form short. Name, phone and one question are often enough.
  8. Set up tracking so you can see which pages bring calls and messages.

A quick mobile test

Open your site on your own phone and try to do what a customer would do: find your service, find the price information and contact you. If any step takes more than a few seconds, fix it first.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Auto-playing music or full-screen pop-ups.
  • Tiny text and tiny buttons.
  • Using only one "Contact" page with no calls to action elsewhere.
  • Leaving broken links or old offers on the site.

What visitors decide in the first few seconds

New visitors scan before they read. They look for three things: am I in the right place, can I trust this business and what do I do next? If the page answers these quickly with a clear headline, real photos, visible contact buttons and a tidy layout, they stay. If not, they press back and open the next result.

Page-by-page improvements

HomeClear headline, short explanation, main button, links to services, proof and contact

AboutReal people and the reasons customers choose you
ContactPhone, WhatsApp, form, map, hours and what happens next

Three scenarios (concepts)

  • A clinic website with only a "Contact" link. Adding appointment buttons on each service page shortens the path to action.
  • A coaching site with large banners. Compressing images and trimming sliders helps it load on ordinary mobile data.
  • A shop site without prices. Adding a "starting from" range or a request-a-quote button helps serious customers decide.

Myths about websites

  • "A beautiful design is enough." Clarity and speed matter more than decoration.
  • "People will scroll to find my number." They will not. Put it where thumbs can reach.
  • "More pages mean more traffic." Only helpful pages do.

A 30-day action plan

  1. Week 1: test your site on your phone, list every problem and fix the contact buttons.
  2. Week 2: rewrite your home headline and each service page introduction.
  3. Week 3: add a short FAQ, real photos and clear next steps.
  4. Week 4: set up tracking for calls, messages and forms and review what changed.

How to measure improvement

Compare enquiries before and after, watch how long visitors stay, which pages they leave from and how many tap your call or WhatsApp buttons. Even small improvements in these numbers show you are on the right track.

Frequently asked questions

How many pages does a small business website need?

Usually a home page, one page per main service, an about page, a contact page and a few helpful guides. Add more only when you have useful things to say.

Should I rebuild or just improve my current website?

If the structure and content are sound, improving is cheaper. If it is slow, confusing and hard to update, a rebuild may be better value.

Will a new website bring me more customers?

A better website helps visitors take action, but you also need visibility so people reach it. Website, SEO and content work best together.
