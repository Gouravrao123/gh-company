Content That Does Not Speak Your Customer's Language: Using English, Hindi and Hinglish

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 4 November 2026

You can have the best service in town and still lose attention if your words feel distant. Here is how to match the way your customers think, talk and search.

What it looks like

  • Your posts sound like a brochure while customers speak casually.
  • Reels have no subtitles, so viewers scroll past.
  • Your website uses terms customers never type into Google.
  • You get fewer replies in comments than you expect.

Why it happens

Marketing content is often copied from templates written for another market. Formal English can feel safe, but it may also feel cold to people who talk in Hindi or Hinglish every day.

What it costs you

People understand and trust what sounds like them. If your words feel foreign, fewer people stop, save, share or ask a question.

How to fix it

  1. Listen to your customers. Note the exact words they use on calls and in WhatsApp messages.
  2. Choose a language mix for each channel. Clear English for your website, Hinglish for captions and reels, Hindi where your audience prefers it.
  3. Add subtitles to every video. Many people watch without sound.
  4. Use short sentences and everyday words.
  5. Match search phrases. If people search "website kitne mein banti hai", answer that question directly.
  6. Keep brand voice consistent. Friendly, clear and respectful works across languages.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Using heavy slang that your audience may not use.
  • Mixing languages randomly in a single sentence.
  • Translating word for word.
  • Forgetting accessibility: readable text size and contrast.

Why language shapes trust

People feel more comfortable with words they use themselves. Plain English, Hindi or Hinglish helps customers relax, ask questions and share your content with friends. It does not mean lowering quality. It means making your message easy to receive.

Where to use each language

PlaceSuggested language
Website headings and service pagesClear, simple English, with Hindi or Hinglish where natural
Reel captions and subtitlesHinglish or Hindi, matching how your audience speaks
WhatsApp repliesThe language the customer used first
Google Business ProfileClear English, plus Hindi in services or posts if useful

Three scenarios (concepts)

  • A coaching centre writes parent-facing posts in simple Hindi and student-facing reels in Hinglish.
  • A clinic explains conditions in plain words, avoiding heavy medical language.
  • A shop uses everyday words in offers: "Diwali offer" and "kitne mein" style questions answered directly.

Myths about language

  • "English looks more professional." Clarity looks professional.
  • "Hinglish is unprofessional." Used well, it sounds friendly and human.
  • "One language fits all." Different audiences prefer different mixes.

A 30-day action plan

  1. Week 1: collect twenty real customer phrases from messages and calls.
  2. Week 2: rewrite your website headline, bio and three posts using those words.
  3. Week 3: add subtitles to every video.
  4. Week 4: compare saves, replies and messages with last month.

How to measure improvement

Look for more comments, more replies in the customer's own words and more saves and shares. These show that people understand and relate to what you say.

Frequently asked questions

Should my website be in Hindi or English?

Use the language your customers use. Many businesses keep clear English for main pages and add Hindi or Hinglish for captions, headings and key pages.

Does Google understand Hinglish?

Google can match many Hinglish and Hindi searches. Helpful, natural content usually works better than forcing exact phrases.

How do I find the phrases my customers use?

Check Search Console queries, Google autosuggest, WhatsApp messages and the questions customers ask on calls.
