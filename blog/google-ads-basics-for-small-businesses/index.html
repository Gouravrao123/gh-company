Google Ads Basics for Small Businesses: How Search Ads Work and Where to Start

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 4 November 2026

Google Ads can bring people who are searching for your service right now. It can also waste money if started without a plan. Here are the basics in plain language.

What Google Ads is

Google Ads lets you show ads when people search on Google. If someone searches for a service you offer, your ad can appear above or below the normal results, marked as an ad. Because the person is actively searching, intent is often high. This is different from social ads, where you reach people who were not looking for you.

How you pay

In most search campaigns you pay when someone clicks your ad, not when it is shown. The cost of a click depends on competition for the search, the quality of your ad and page and your settings. There is no fixed price, so a small test and weekly review are the safest way to learn.

Google Ads basics: a first-campaign checklist

  1. Pick one goal, such as calls or form leads.
  2. List ten to twenty specific searches your customers use.
  3. Write two or three ad versions with a clear offer.
  4. Send clicks to a page that matches the ad, not a general home page.
  5. Set a small budget you can afford while learning.
  6. Set up conversion tracking before launch.
  7. Review weekly: search terms, clicks, cost and enquiries.

When Google Ads fits a small business

Search ads suit services people look for when they need them, such as a plumber, a clinic or an urgent repair. They are less suitable if few people search for your offer, in which case social ads or content may work better. Always check that your website or WhatsApp is ready to receive enquiries.

Mistakes beginners make

  • Using broad keywords that attract unrelated searches.
  • Sending everyone to the home page.
  • Not tracking calls and forms.
  • Changing everything at once.
  • Judging results after two days.

A worked example (concept)

A clinic wants more appointment calls. It lists fifteen specific searches such as the treatment name plus the city, writes two ads that repeat the search and mention timings, and sends clicks to a page with a call button. It tracks calls from the ad for three weeks, reads the search terms report, adds irrelevant searches as negatives and pauses the weaker ad. None of this guarantees results, but each step makes the next decision easier.

A 30-day starter plan

  1. Week 1: choose the goal, list searches and prepare the landing page.
  2. Week 2: launch a small campaign with tracking.
  3. Week 3: review search terms and add negatives.
  4. Week 4: compare ads, pause the weaker and decide the next month’s budget.

Frequently asked questions

Is Google Ads good for a local business?

It can be, especially for services people search for when they need them. Start with a small, tracked test and a ready landing page.

How much should I spend on Google Ads?

Start with an amount you can afford to lose while you learn, and increase only when you can see what works.

Do I need a website for Google Ads?

A clear landing page usually helps conversion and tracking, although some campaigns send people to a phone call or messaging.
