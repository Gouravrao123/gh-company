How Much Should a Small Business Spend on Ads? A PPC Advertising Guide

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 4 November 2026

There is no single number, but there is a sensible method. Here is a simple PPC advertising guide for planning your first budget.

Key takeaways

  • Choose one goal
  • Choose a small local audience
  • Set a test budget
  • Prepare two or three creatives

The short answer

Start with an amount you can afford to lose while learning, spread over a few weeks. Decide in advance what result would justify spending more, and keep ad spend separate from agency fees.

Why business owners ask this

Owners fear overspending without results or underspending and learning nothing.

What affects the answer

Budget depends on your goal, area, competition, offer and conversion process. PPC means you pay when people see or click ads, depending on the platform and settings.

Step by step: find your own answer

  1. Choose one goal, such as WhatsApp messages.
  2. Choose a small local audience.
  3. Set a test budget for two to four weeks.
  4. Prepare two or three creatives.
  5. Track enquiries and cost.
  6. Increase only after evidence.

Useful things to check

  • Cost per enquiry
  • Click-to-enquiry rate
  • Enquiry-to-customer rate
  • Return on spend

A concept example

A salon tests a small budget on a local audience for three weeks. It finds one creative brings more bookings and moves the budget to it. This is an illustration, not a client result.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Starting too big.
  • Judging after two days.
  • Targeting too wide.
  • Forgetting follow-up.

Worked example: a four-week test plan

A salon sets a small test budget, divides it across four weeks and runs two creatives to a local audience with a WhatsApp button. In week one it checks that tracking works. In weeks two and three it compares messages per creative. In week four it pauses the weaker creative and decides whether to continue, using the cost per message and how many became bookings.

The numbers are illustrative, but the structure applies to any business: small budget, one goal, two creatives, weekly review and a decision rule written in advance, such as “continue only if the cost per booking is acceptable”.

Myths vs reality

MythReality
More budget fixes everythingOffer and follow-up matter too

A 30-day action plan

  1. Week 1: Define goal and budget.
  2. Week 2: Launch a small test.
  3. Week 3: Compare creatives weekly.
  4. Week 4: Decide the next month’s budget.

How to measure improvement

Compare cost per enquiry and per customer after each cycle.

Is boosting a post enough?

It is simpler but offers fewer options.

Ad spend is usually paid directly to the platform.

How long should I test?

A few weeks, depending on volume.
