How Business Owners in India Search for Marketing Help: Hindi, Hinglish and English Phrases

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 4 November 2026

"Dukaan ka promotion kaise kare" or "digital marketing agency in Rewari"? People search the way they talk. Here are the phrase types, what they mean and how to use them naturally on your website and posts.

People rarely search the way marketers write. A shop owner in Rewari is more likely to type or say "dukaan ka promotion kaise kare" than "retail brand awareness strategy". If your website, Google profile and social posts only use formal English, you may be missing the way real customers think. This guide explains the patterns of Hindi, Hinglish and English phrases business owners use, what each type of phrase usually means and where to use it.

A note on honesty: the phrases below are examples of common patterns, not a list of verified search volumes. Check real data for your own business in Google Search Console, Google autosuggest and the questions your customers ask you.

Why the language of your content matters

Search engines understand Hindi, Hinglish (Hindi written in English letters) and English, and many people mix them in the same sentence. When your page answers a question in the words the customer uses, it is easier to match and easier to trust. It also feels friendlier. The aim is not to stuff keywords. It is to write the way your customer speaks, clearly and helpfully.

Question words that signal intent

  • Kaise (how): the person wants steps. Give a clear numbered guide.
  • Kya (what): the person wants a definition. Explain simply, for example "SEO kya hota hai", and give an example.
  • Kitna (how much): the person wants cost or time. Give ranges and say what changes them.
  • Kab and kitne din (when, how long): the person wants a timeline. Be honest about uncertainty.
  • Kahan (where): the person wants a location or place to start. Local pages and Google Maps content fit here.
  • Kya sahi hai (is it right or worth it): the person wants a comparison or opinion. Offer a fair table and a clear recommendation with reasons.

English words inside Hindi sentences

Many searches mix English business words with Hindi grammar: "SEO kya hota hai", "reels kaise banaye", "website kitne mein banti hai", "Instagram par followers kaise badhaye". Some people also type in Devanagari, such as "डिजिटल मार्केटिंग क्या है" or "वेबसाइट कितने में बनती है". You do not need to translate your whole site. Use clear English for the main content and add Hindi or Hinglish where it helps: in question headings, in captions, in reel subtitles and in WhatsApp replies.

How to use these phrases without stuffing

  • Write headings as the question people ask, then answer it in two or three clear lines.
  • Use one main phrase per page and let related phrases appear naturally.
  • Do not repeat the same phrase unnaturally. If it reads strangely aloud, rewrite it.
  • Add Hinglish subtitles to reels, because many people watch without sound.
  • Use the same wording in your Google Business Profile description and on your website.
  • Keep spelling consistent, but accept that Hinglish spellings vary: "kaise" and "kese" are both common.

Example: turning a phrase into a useful page

Take the phrase "dukaan ka promotion kaise kare". A good page would:

  1. Open with a one-line answer: start with Google Maps, photos and WhatsApp before spending on ads.
  2. List five practical steps with a short example for a shop in a small city.
  3. Include a small table comparing free steps and paid steps.
  4. Mention what it costs in time and what it can cost in money.
  5. End with a simple next step, such as checking your Google profile or contacting a local expert.

Phrases around online visibility

Owners who say "Google par business kaise dikhaye" or "online visibility kaise badhaye" are asking the same thing as "how to improve online visibility": they want to be seen when customers search. The honest answer is a list: a complete Google Business Profile, a clear website with a page per service, useful local content, genuine reviews and consistent social activity. Where to find the phrases your customers really use

  • Google Search Console: the Queries report shows the exact searches that led to your site.
  • Google autosuggest and "People also ask": type the start of a question and see how Google completes it.
  • Your own WhatsApp and call history: the way customers phrase their first message is often the best keyword research.
  • Instagram and YouTube search suggestions: useful for short-video topics.
  • Google Business Profile insights: shows the search terms people used to find your listing.

Frequently asked questions

Should I write my website in Hindi, English or Hinglish?

Use the language your customers use. Clear English works well for main pages in many cities, with Hindi or Hinglish for captions, subtitles, headings and WhatsApp replies. A Hindi version of key pages can help if many of your customers prefer Hindi.

Does Google understand Hinglish searches?

Google can match many Hinglish and Hindi queries, though results vary. Writing helpful content that uses natural wording usually works better than forcing exact phrases.

Can you give me search volume numbers for these phrases?

Not without checking real tools. Volumes differ by city and change over time. Use Search Console and Google Keyword Planner for your own area, or ask us to include it in your SEO plan.
