Enquiries Arrive but Do Not Become Customers: Fix the Follow-Up

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 4 November 2026

Marketing brings the enquiry. What happens in the next hour often decides whether it becomes a customer. Here is how to fix the part after the click.

What it looks like

  • Messages sit unanswered for hours.
  • People ask for the price and then go quiet.
  • Different staff give different answers.
  • Nobody follows up after the first reply.

Why it happens

Enquiries arrive while the team is busy serving existing customers. There is no process, so replies depend on who happens to see the message. Owners then blame the leads or the ads, when the real gap is in the response.

What it costs you

A person who messages three businesses will often reply to the first one that answers clearly. A slow reply can turn a paid enquiry into a wasted one. Improving follow-up is often the cheapest way to get more customers from the same marketing.

How to fix it

  1. Choose who replies. One person per shift, with a backup.
  2. Set a reply target. For example, within an hour during working hours, and say your hours on your profile.
  3. Prepare short answers for common questions: price range, timings, location, process, next step.
  4. Use a greeting message on WhatsApp Business so people know they are heard.
  5. Ask one qualifying question early, such as what the person needs and by when.
  6. Follow up politely once or twice if there is no reply. Keep it helpful, not pushy.
  7. Record the outcome of each enquiry so you can see what stops people from buying.

A simple follow-up message (concept)

"Hello, thank you for your interest in [service]. I wanted to check if you still need help with this and whether you have any questions about timing or price. Happy to explain."

Mistakes to avoid

  • Sending long walls of text as the first reply.
  • Hiding the price range completely.
  • Pressuring people with repeated messages.
  • Ignoring leads that said "not now".

What happens in the first hour

Someone sees your ad or post, taps the button and sends a message. At that moment their interest is highest. They are probably messaging more than one business. The one that replies clearly, kindly and quickly often wins even if it is not the cheapest. Preparing for that hour is part of your marketing.

A simple enquiry journey

Three scenarios (concepts)

  • A gym with slow replies in the evening. Setting one person to check messages at fixed times can recover many lost enquiries.
  • A coaching centre with different answers from staff. A shared answer sheet creates consistency.
  • A clinic with no follow-up. A friendly reminder message for people who asked but did not book can reopen conversations.

Myths about leads

  • "Bad leads are the ad's fault." Some are, but slow or unclear replies lose good ones too.
  • "Following up is pushy." Respectful follow-up is service.
  • "Hiding the price keeps people interested." It often makes them leave.

A 30-day action plan

  1. Week 1: assign reply responsibility and write ready answers.
  2. Week 2: set up WhatsApp Business greetings and quick replies.
  3. Week 3: start a simple lead sheet with date, source, status and next step.
  4. Week 4: review which enquiries converted and which stopped, and note why.

How to measure improvement

Track the time to first reply, the share of enquiries that receive a follow-up and the share that become customers. Improvements in these three usually come before any increase in ad spend.

Frequently asked questions

How fast should I reply to an enquiry?

As fast as you realistically can, ideally within an hour during working hours. Set expectations clearly if you are unavailable.

Should I share prices in the first message?

Sharing a range or a starting point usually saves time for both sides and attracts serious buyers. Explain what changes the final price.

How many times should I follow up?

One or two polite follow-ups are reasonable. Stop if the person says no or asks not to be contacted.
