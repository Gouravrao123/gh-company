Fake or Unverifiable Agency Reviews and Portfolios: How to Check Before You Trust

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 4 November 2026

Perfect five-star testimonials and anonymous case studies can hide a lot. Here is how to verify an agency’s work before you hire.

Key takeaways

  • Read reviews for specifics
  • Check reviewer profiles
  • Read critical reviews
  • Ask for live examples

What this frustration looks like

  • All reviews are five stars with similar wording.
  • Case studies have no names, dates or context.
  • Awards and rankings come from unknown sites.
  • Logos of famous brands appear without explanation.

Why it happens

Reviews and portfolios influence decisions, so some agencies polish or invent them. Directories sometimes sell rankings.

What it costs you

Choosing based on fake proof leads to poor fit, wasted money and distrust.

How to avoid it

  1. Read reviews for specifics: service, situation and results.
  2. Check reviewer profiles and whether the business exists.
  3. Read critical reviews and how the agency responded.
  4. Ask for live examples you can open, with permission.
  5. Ask for a reference you can call.
  6. Treat paid ranking lists carefully.

Questions to ask any agency

  • Can I see live work or accounts you manage?
  • Can I speak to a past or current client?
  • How were these results measured?
  • Are examples real or concepts?

A concept example

An agency shows three identical testimonials with no names. Asking to speak to one client ends the conversation, which tells you something. This is an illustration, not a client result.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Trusting star ratings alone.
  • Not checking links.
  • Ignoring unclear case studies.
  • Believing rankings without knowing who made them.

Myths vs reality

MythReality
Many reviews mean qualityQuantity can be manufactured
Big logos prove experienceAsk what work was done
Awards guarantee resultsCheck who awards them

A 30-day action plan

  1. Week 1: Shortlist two agencies.
  2. Week 2: Check their live work and reviews.
  3. Week 3: Ask the verification questions above.
  4. Week 4: Choose the one that answers openly.

How to measure improvement

You are done when you have verified at least one piece of real work and spoken to or read a detailed review about each shortlisted agency.

Frequently asked questions

How can I spot fake reviews?

Look for generic wording, similar dates, missing details and reviewer profiles with no history.

Is a concept portfolio acceptable?

Yes, if it is clearly labelled as concept work.

What proof should I ask for?

Live work, a reference and a clear explanation of how results were measured.
