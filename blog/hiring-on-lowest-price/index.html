Mistake: Hiring on the Lowest Price or the Biggest Promise

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 4 November 2026

The cheapest offer often leaves things out, and the biggest promise is a warning sign. Here is how to compare fairly.

Key takeaways

  • Compare scope
  • Ask about process and reporting
  • Check references
  • Compare terms

What this mistake looks like

  • Choosing the cheapest quote.
  • Being drawn by guarantees.
  • Not comparing scope.
  • Ignoring communication.

Why businesses make it

Budgets are tight and promises are attractive.

What it costs you

You may pay twice: once for the wrong partner and again for the right one.

What to do instead

  1. Compare scope.
  2. Ask about process and reporting.
  3. Check references.
  4. Compare terms.
  5. Look at communication.
  6. Start small.

A quick self-check

  • What is included in each quote?
  • What is not guaranteed?
  • How will results be reported?
  • Can I speak to a client?

A concept example

Two quotes differ by 30 percent. The higher one includes reporting and revisions that the lower one charges extra for. This is an illustration, not a client result.

Related mistakes

  • Price-only decisions.
  • Believing guarantees.
  • Skipping references.
  • Ignoring terms.

Worked example: comparing three quotes

A shop receives three quotes: a low price with unclear scope, a mid price with listed deliverables and reporting, and a high price with guarantees. The owner builds a table: deliverables, exclusions, reporting, term, ownership of accounts and communication. The low quote excludes revisions and reporting, the high quote relies on a guarantee with conditions and the mid quote is clear.

The mid quote is not chosen because it is in the middle. It is chosen because the owner can explain exactly what will be delivered and how it will be judged.

Myths vs reality

MythReality
Cheapest saves moneyNot always
Big promises show confidenceThey may hide risks
Small agencies are riskyJudge by process

A 30-day action plan

  1. Week 1: Make a comparison table.
  2. Week 2: Ask questions.
  3. Week 3: Check references.
  4. Week 4: Start with a limited scope.

How to measure improvement

Judge after the first month on communication and deliverables.
