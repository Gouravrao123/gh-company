“I Am Not Good With Technology, So I Will Be Cheated”: How to Stay in Control

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 4 November 2026

You do not need to understand every tool. You need a clear scope, plain-language reports and the right questions.

Key takeaways

  • Ask for a written scope
  • Ask for plain-language reports
  • Ask what each task is for
  • Keep ownership of your accounts

How this belief sounds

  • “I cannot judge their work.”
  • “They will use big words and I will agree.”
  • “If something goes wrong, I will not know.”

Where it comes from

Technology language feels like a locked room. Stories of bad agencies make caution understandable.

What it costs you

Fear can lead to either avoiding marketing or handing over everything without questions, and both are risky.

A truer way to look at it

  1. Ask for a written scope in simple language.
  2. Ask for plain-language reports.
  3. Ask what each task is for.
  4. Keep ownership of your accounts.
  5. Start small and review.
  6. Bring a trusted person to meetings if it helps.

Small first steps

  • Can you explain this in simple words?
  • Who owns the accounts?
  • What will you report?
  • What are the terms?

A concept example

A shop owner asks an agency to explain each line of the plan in everyday words and decides to proceed only after understanding it. This is an illustration, not a client result.

What to avoid

  • Signing without understanding.
  • Not asking questions.
  • Giving away passwords.
  • Skipping the written scope.

Five questions that protect you without technical knowledge

Ask: what exactly will you do each month, what will you report and how often, who owns the accounts, what is excluded from the price and how can I stop the service? You do not need to understand the tools to judge whether the answers are specific, written and consistent.

If an agency cannot or will not answer in plain language, that is information too. Many owners find that a short written scope and a monthly review call remove most of the fear.

Myths vs reality

MythReality
Only experts can judgeClear questions are enough
Jargon means expertisePlain language shows it
Asking is embarrassingGood partners welcome it

A 30-day action plan

  1. Week 1: List your questions.
  2. Week 2: Ask for plain-language explanations.
  3. Week 3: Request a written scope.
  4. Week 4: Review after the first month.

How to measure improvement

You are in control when you can explain what your agency does in two sentences.
