“Agencies Only Want My Money”: How to Tell the Difference

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 4 November 2026

Some agencies do behave that way. The difference shows in how they answer questions about scope, ownership and promises.

Key takeaways

  • Ask for a discovery conversation
  • Request a written proposal
  • Ask about process and reporting
  • Compare more than one agency

How this belief sounds

  • “Every call ends with a package price.”
  • “They do not care about my business.”
  • “They all say the same thing.”

Where it comes from

Aggressive sales and vague promises build distrust, and many owners have heard both.

What it costs you

Distrust may push you to avoid help you need or to choose the least engaged option.

A truer way to look at it

  1. Ask for a discovery conversation.
  2. Request a written proposal.
  3. Ask about process and reporting.
  4. Compare more than one agency.
  5. Check references.
  6. Start with a limited scope.

Small first steps

  • What do you need to know about my business?
  • What is not included?
  • How will you report?
  • What if I stop?

A concept example

A shop owner compares two proposals. One asks about customers and goals, the other sends a package list. The first becomes the choice. This is an illustration, not a client result.

What to avoid

  • Choosing on friendliness only.
  • Ignoring terms.
  • Accepting urgency.
  • Not asking questions.

What a respectful sales conversation sounds like

A good first call includes questions about your customers, your goals and what you have tried. The agency explains how it would approach your situation, what it needs from you and what it cannot promise. It offers to send a written proposal and gives you time to compare.

If instead you hear price first, urgency and guarantees, you have learned something useful about how the relationship may feel later. Trust your instincts and use the questions in this guide.

A 30-day action plan

  1. Week 1: List your questions.
  2. Week 2: Request proposals.
  3. Week 3: Compare.
  4. Week 4: Start small.

How to measure improvement

Judge by clarity, responsiveness and the match between proposal and your goals.
