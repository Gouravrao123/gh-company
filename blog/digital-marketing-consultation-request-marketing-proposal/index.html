Digital Marketing Consultation: How to Prepare and Request a Marketing Proposal

By Gourav Yadav, GH Company, Rewari · Published 4 November 2026

Hiring a digital marketing agency should start with understanding your business, not immediately buying a package. A useful consultation helps you explain your goals, identify marketing gaps and understand what an agency can realistically do. This guide explains how to prepare for a consultation, request a written proposal and compare agencies before making a decision.

What does a digital marketing consultation cover?

A digital marketing consultation is an initial discussion between a business and a marketing professional or agency. Its purpose is to understand the business situation, identify priorities and decide whether professional marketing support is appropriate.

A useful consultation should not be only a sales pitch. It should give both sides enough information to understand the problem, possible solutions, expected responsibilities and next steps.

A consultation with a digital marketing agency may cover:

  • Your business and customers: What you sell, who you serve, your location and what makes your business different.
  • Your marketing goals: More enquiries, website visits, local visibility, appointments, sales or stronger brand awareness.
  • Your current online presence: Website, Google Business Profile, social media accounts and existing campaigns.
  • Your previous marketing efforts: What you have tried, what worked and where you faced difficulties.
  • Your competitors: How customers discover similar businesses and what opportunities may be available.
  • Your budget and priorities: Which activities are realistic within your available resources.
  • Next steps: Whether an audit, strategy discussion or written proposal would be useful.

For businesses in Rewari and other parts of Haryana, the discussion may also cover local search visibility, service-area targeting, customer reviews and how potential customers discover nearby businesses.

How to prepare for a digital marketing consultation

You do not need a complete marketing strategy before contacting an agency. However, preparing a few basic details can make the first conversation more useful and help the agency understand your requirements.

  • Choose one main goal: Decide what matters most over the next 90 days. For example, more qualified enquiries or better local visibility.
  • Collect your online links: Keep your website, Instagram, Facebook and Google Business Profile links ready.
  • Understand your customers: Think about your ideal customer, location, age group where relevant and buying requirements.
  • Review your current marketing: Note your existing posts, advertisements, website traffic or enquiry sources if available.
  • Set a realistic budget range: You do not need an exact figure, but a range helps the agency recommend suitable options.
  • Prepare your questions: Ask about deliverables, timelines, reporting, account ownership and payment terms.

If you do not have analytics data or marketing reports, say so. A responsible agency should explain what information is available, what is missing and whether tracking needs to be configured.

What information should you share with a digital marketing agency?

The quality of a proposal depends partly on the information available during the discovery conversation. You can prepare the following details before requesting a quote.

  • Business name and type of business.
  • City, service area or locations you want to target.
  • Main products or services you want to promote.
  • Your ideal customers and the problems your business solves.
  • Website and social media profile links, if available.
  • Current marketing activities and previous campaign experience.
  • Your main business objective and approximate budget.
  • Any important deadlines, seasonal offers or upcoming launches.

Do not share account passwords in an ordinary consultation message. If an agency needs access later, use the platform's official permission and role-management features wherever possible.

How to request a digital marketing proposal

Once you have explained your business requirements, ask the agency for a written proposal. A written document makes it easier to understand exactly what is being offered and compare it with other agencies.

  1. Contact the agency: Use its official website, business email, contact form or WhatsApp number.
  2. Explain your business: Share your business type, location, services and target customers.
  3. Describe your goal: Explain what you want to improve and why it matters to your business.
  4. Share available links and information: Provide your website, social profiles and any relevant marketing data.
  5. Discuss your budget and expectations: Be clear about your approximate spending capacity and priorities.
  6. Have a discovery conversation: Give the agency an opportunity to ask questions before recommending services.
  7. Request a written proposal: Ask for scope, deliverables, timelines, pricing, exclusions and terms.
  8. Review before accepting: Ask for clarification and compare the proposal with other suitable options.

What should a good digital marketing agency proposal include?

A professional proposal should make the work understandable before you agree to pay for it. Avoid accepting a vague document that only lists service names and a total price.

Use this checklist when reviewing a digital marketing proposal:

  • Business understanding: Does the agency explain your business, target audience and marketing objective correctly?
  • Recommended strategy: Does it explain why particular channels or services are being recommended?
  • Defined scope: Are the services clearly listed rather than described with broad terms such as complete marketing?
  • Measurable deliverables: Are the number of posts, videos, campaigns, pages or other outputs specified where applicable?
  • Timeline: Does the proposal explain the initial work, milestones and review schedule?
  • Pricing: Are service fees, advertising budgets, taxes and additional charges clearly distinguished?
  • Reporting: Does it explain which performance metrics will be shared and how often?
  • Responsibilities: Does it specify what the agency will do and what information or approvals it needs from you?
  • Account ownership: Will your business retain ownership and appropriate access to its website, advertising accounts and social profiles?
  • Terms: Are payment schedules, cancellation conditions, revisions and other relevant terms documented?

A proposal should also explain what is not included. For example, a Meta Ads management fee is not necessarily the same as the advertising budget paid to the platform.

How to compare digital marketing agency proposals

Do not choose an agency only because it offers the lowest price or promises the highest number of leads. Compare the actual work, clarity and suitability of each proposal.

What to compare What to look for
Strategy A plan connected to your business goals and target customers.
Deliverables Specific outputs, quantities and responsibilities.
Pricing Clear fees, advertising spend and additional costs.
Reporting Relevant metrics, reporting frequency and tracking limitations.
Communication A clear contact person, approval process and response expectations.
Ownership Your business retains control of its important digital assets.
Terms Written payment, cancellation, renewal and handover conditions.

Ask every agency to quote against the same basic requirements where possible. This makes comparisons fairer and helps you identify differences in scope that may explain price differences.

When should you choose a full-service digital marketing agency?

A full-service digital marketing agency can be useful when your business needs multiple connected services rather than one isolated activity. For example, a business may need a website, search visibility, social media content and paid advertising support.

These activities can support each other when they are planned around a shared business objective. However, not every business needs every service immediately. A focused starting plan may be more practical when your budget or internal capacity is limited.

A practical example: preparing for a gym marketing consultation

Imagine a gym owner in Rewari who wants more trial registrations and local enquiries. Before contacting an agency, the owner prepares three things:

  • The goal: Increase relevant trial-session enquiries from people in the nearby service area.
  • Existing online presence: Website link, Instagram profile and available information about previous campaigns.
  • Budget and requirements: An approximate budget and the services the gym is interested in discussing.

During the consultation, the agency asks about the gym's location, membership options, target audience, current enquiry process and available creative material. Based on the discussion, it may recommend a suitable combination of local visibility, social media content and advertising.

The owner then receives a written proposal showing the recommended work, deliverables, timeline, fees and any separate advertising budget. The owner can review the scope, ask questions and compare the proposal before deciding.

This is an illustrative example, not a promise of a particular number of leads or memberships. Actual results depend on the offer, market, competition, budget, campaign execution and enquiry handling.

Questions to ask during a digital marketing consultation

Use these questions to understand whether an agency's approach is suitable for your business.

  1. What do you understand about my business and target customers?
  2. Which marketing activities would you prioritise first, and why?
  3. What will you deliver during the first month?
  4. How will you measure progress towards my business goal?
  5. Which reports or performance updates will I receive?
  6. What is included in the quoted price, and what is excluded?
  7. Is the advertising budget separate from your management fee?
  8. What access or material will you need from my team?
  9. Who will own the website, advertising accounts and creative assets?
  10. What are the payment, cancellation and handover terms?

Mistakes to avoid before hiring a digital marketing agency

  • Choosing only by price: A low quote may exclude important work or provide a smaller scope.
  • Accepting vague promises: Ask for measurable deliverables instead of guaranteed sales, rankings or leads.
  • Skipping the written proposal: Verbal discussions can leave room for misunderstandings.
  • Ignoring exclusions: Confirm whether ad spend, website hosting, domain costs, tools and taxes are included.
  • Not checking ownership: Keep appropriate ownership and administrative access to your business accounts.
  • Expecting instant results: Different marketing activities have different timelines and dependencies.
  • Paying without understanding the terms: Review the scope, payment schedule and cancellation conditions before accepting.

A consultation is an opportunity to evaluate the agency as well as explain your requirements. You should feel comfortable asking questions and taking time to review the proposal.
